# Discovery Feature vs. Deal Pilot Protocol

## Objective

Test H-001 and H-002 with five willing Bowling Green merchants. Do not publish the pilot until each merchant has confirmed factual details, use permission, and reshare contact.

## Pair design

For each merchant, create two posts in the same approximate two-week window:

1. **Discovery feature:** a real person/place-led story that answers why a resident should care or what a first-timer should know.
2. **Deal-led control:** a clear offer with the same merchant, comparable call-to-action, and straightforward visual.

Avoid claiming the two posts are scientifically identical. Document unavoidable differences: timing, creative, merchant audience size, seasonal relevance, and paid reach (which should be zero for this first organic test).

## Merchant selection guardrails

Choose a mix of businesses that can provide original visuals and a genuine local detail: one food/experience, one family/activities, one retail, one personal/service, and one niche/interest business. Exclude any merchant whose only material is a generic discount graphic. The current owner-verified slate and individualized drafts are in `pilot-candidates.md`.

## Measurement

Record 24-hour, 72-hour, and 7-day views/reach, reactions, comments, shares, saves if exposed, profile visits, link clicks, follows, merchant reshares, and inquiries. Code comment quality: planning question, tag/share cue, purchase intent, generic praise, or unrelated. Capture screenshots/URLs where allowed.

## Decision rule

Do not declare a winner from a single post. Assess the direction across all pairs, separate results by merchant/category, and decide whether to repeat, modify, or reject the hypothesis. A merchant reshare is a distribution outcome, not evidence of consumer preference by itself.

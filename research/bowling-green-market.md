# Bowling Green Facebook Attention: Seed Synthesis

## Observed evidence

The inherited raw archive contains 141 meaningful observations, 29 source accounts/communities, and 78 named businesses/organizations (some are organizations rather than commercial businesses). Most were collected in the primary recent window. The sample is broad but incomplete; it does **not** meet the original saturation target of 250 observations, 40 sources, 50 business pages examined, or a consistently documented 30-comment-inspection threshold.

Visible standouts include Jackson's Orchard Apple Fest food preview (373 reactions, 64 comments, 100 shares), its information-rich planning post (121/13/29), a City Parks big-events roundup (45/3/47), and free-family/school event posts with high sharing relative to source size. Observed smaller but useful conversations included splash-pad status questions and local restaurant recommendations.

Within-source comparisons also matter: staff humor outperformed static product education at Morris Jewelry; coworker humor outperformed generic programming at Bowling Green Athletic Club; Hot Rods playoff urgency/humor exceeded ordinary update posts. Generic newsletters, vague seasonal teasers, routine closures, and generic inventory frequently had low visible interaction.

## Interpretation — moderate confidence

Bowling Green Facebook appears to reward content that reduces a near-term planning decision, makes a local ritual tangible, or gives people a locally recognizable personality/moment to share. This is not evidence that every family, food, sports, or humor post will work, and engagement is not purchase intent.

## Implications to test

Use discovery content that is concrete: exact experience, what to expect, who it is for, why now, and a simple reason to tag or share. Couple merchant exposure with reader utility. Compare a human/story feature against a conventional deal graphic, measuring shares, comment quality, merchant reshares, profile visits, and earned followers.

## Known sampling bias

Facebook feed availability and account access shape the sample. Groups and Facebook are oversampled relative to Instagram, LinkedIn, comparable-market discovery brands, and internal performance. Public-safety attention is observed ecosystem context, not proposed content territory.
